01 / The practice

Style is not a costume. It is editing — until what remains is unmistakably you.

Makeover Mentor is a slow, personal practice. We begin with colour, light and the shape of a face, then work outward — cut, wardrobe, presence.

Nothing is applied from a trend. Everything is chosen for one person, in one room, at one pace.

03 / The method

Four movements

A day, arranged in order. Each part earns the next.

01 — Read

We look before we touch

Bone structure, skin, the way light falls across a face in the morning. Nothing is decided yet. This is the quietest hour of the day.

02 — Define

A palette and a shape, agreed

We settle the colour direction and the silhouette before a single cut is made. Every choice from here has a reason we can both point to.

03 — Make

The long, unhurried work

Cut, colour, makeup — carried out slowly, in sequence, with time to look in between. The transformation is a process, not a reveal.

04 — Keep

How to repeat it without us

The part that lasts: the products, the order, the two-minute version for a Tuesday. You leave able to do it yourself.

06 / Selected work

Studies

Three approaches, photographed plainly. The craft notes sit beside the image, not on top of it.

Study 01 — Hair

Warm dimension, built in three passes

A base, a mid-tone, a light. Layers are placed where the hair already wants to fall, so the movement survives a windy afternoon and the colour survives a winter.

Study 02 — Makeup

Skin first, everything after

Most of the hour is spent on skin. Colour is placed sparingly on top — a warm shadow, a defined lash line — so the face reads as itself at close range and in daylight.

Study 03 — Colour

A palette tested in real light

Fabric is held against the face at a window, not under a shop light. The colours that stay are the ones that flatter you in the rooms you actually stand in.

08 / The mentor

The work is one-to-one, and it stays that way.

Makeover Mentor is a personal styling and image practice. Every appointment is a single conversation about one person — what they wear, how they move, what they want to be recognised for.

There is no team behind a screen and no house style imposed from above. The eye in the room is the whole product.
